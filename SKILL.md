# Lev Presentations

Create decks that feel calm, warm, and deliberate: dark water, moss, paper, and a rare petal-colour accent. Prefer clarity over decoration. Apply this skill as the single source of truth for Lev's presentation style.

## Start

For a new deck, establish the topic, audience, learning goal, delivery format, required sources, and output folder. Ask only for missing decisions that materially change the result. Do not invent a teacher's name, course name, citations, or facts.

For an existing deck, inspect it before editing. Preserve a usable theme, masters, layout, and editable content whenever possible.

## Visual system

Use this palette deliberately:

| Role | Colour | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep base | `#0A3323` | Cover, section slides, key fields, dark text on light backgrounds |
| Moss | `#839958` | Lines, labels, quiet data accents, secondary elements |
| Paper | `#F7F4D5` | Primary light background, cards, breathing room |
| Petal | `#D3968C` | One focal number, quote, warning, or human detail; never every slide |
| Midnight water | `#105666` | Secondary highlight, diagrams, charts, contrast blocks |

Keep most content slides light (`#F7F4D5`) with deep-green typography. Use dark slides only for the cover, section transitions, and one strong conclusion. Avoid generic gradients, glossy stock imagery, dense card grids, decoration without meaning, and more than two accent colours on a slide.

Use still-life or botanical imagery only when it supports the topic; do not turn every deck into a floral motif. Favour quiet editorial composition, generous margins, one visual hierarchy, and diagrams that explain relationships at a glance.

## Typography

Use **Cormorant Garamond** as the accent face: cover title, section title, short quotation, or one large idea. Keep it large and sparse; never use it for paragraphs, tables, chart labels, or dense technical text.

Use **Manrope** as the stable face: body copy, data, navigation, captions, and all dense material. Use 400–500 for reading, 600–700 for emphasis. If these fonts are unavailable, use **Georgia** instead of Cormorant Garamond and **Montserrat** instead of Manrope. Do not mix a third typeface.

Maintain a strong size contrast: title approximately 2.5–3× body size. Use short, meaningful headings rather than category labels. Ensure Cyrillic is supported and fully embedded or substituted before delivering PPTX/PDF files.

## Make the content easy

Build a visible argument, not a transcript:

- Give each slide one job and one takeaway.
- Start from an everyday intuition, then show the mechanism, then the consequence or example.
- Convert lists into comparisons, sequences, maps, or one annotated visual when this makes the relationship clearer.
- Split a dense thought across slides instead of shrinking text.
- Put a direct answer on the next slide after every audience question.

Write in plain Russian. Use short active sentences, precise nouns, concrete verbs, examples, numbers, and ordinary words. Remove filler and AI-sounding constructions: «в современном мире», «важно отметить», «стоит подчеркнуть», «давайте погрузимся», «осуществлять», «данный», «является», «в рамках», «инсайты», «таким образом, мы видим». Do not replace them mechanically: rewrite until the sentence sounds natural when spoken aloud.

Avoid stacked buzzwords, ceremonial conclusions, empty superlatives, and symmetrical three-item lists. Keep terminology only where it teaches something. For Russian text, use consistent «ёлочки», long dashes with spaces, and one number/unit style.

## Build and verify

Prefer native editable text, shapes, charts, and tables in PPTX. Never render a full slide as a bitmap to fake editability. Export PDFs with selectable text.

Before delivery:

1. Render every slide and inspect the whole deck for clipping, overlap, contrast, empty space, alignment, and readable small text.
2. Extract or reread the final rendered slide text, not only the source, and complete a separate language pass.
3. Fix every remaining typo, awkward phrase, cliché, unexplained abbreviation, and AI-sounding sentence; rebuild and recheck if anything changed.

Deliver the requested format and briefly state what was created and what was verified. If no suitable authoring or rendering tool is available, say so plainly and produce the best editable source available.
